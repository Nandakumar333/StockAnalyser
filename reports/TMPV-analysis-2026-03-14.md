# Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Limited (TMPV) Fundamental Analysis

## 1. Company overview

Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Limited is an Indian automobile manufacturer operating in passenger cars, SUVs, EVs, spare parts, accessories, engines for industrial applications, factory automation equipment, and related services such as vehicle financing and insurance broking. Yahoo Finance describes it as operating across India, China, the United States, the United Kingdom, Europe, and other international markets, while Screener highlights that the business remains part of the broader Tata Motors automotive ecosystem.

The business is primarily B2C in end demand, but its economic model is more complex than a plain domestic car maker because revenue and profitability are influenced by global auto cycles, premium vehicle demand, EV penetration, and international operations. Screener's segment note indicates Jaguar Land Rover accounted for 71% of 9M FY25 revenue versus 67% in FY22, which means the company is not just an India passenger-vehicle story. Export and overseas exposure are meaningful, so currency moves, global demand, and trade policy matter.

## 3. Business model and segment analysis

Screener's segment note shows Jaguar Land Rover is the dominant revenue contributor at 71% of 9M FY25 revenue, up from 67% in FY22. That means valuation and earnings are still heavily influenced by the premium global auto cycle rather than only domestic passenger vehicle sales.

Core revenue drivers appear to be:

| Segment / driver | Relevance |
| --- | --- |
| Jaguar Land Rover | Primary revenue engine and a major source of global exposure |
| Domestic passenger vehicles and SUVs | Important volume base in India |
| EV portfolio | Strategic growth driver, especially in India |
| Spares, accessories, financing, insurance, and related services | Ancillary support revenue |

This is a moderately high-complexity business. The domestic PV and EV story is easy to understand, but group economics are tied to premium auto demand, product mix, commodity costs, forex, and international execution. That makes TMPV harder to underwrite than a simple asset-light consumer company.

The multi-year picture is mixed but not weak. Screener reports compounded sales growth of 10% over 5 years and 14% over 3 years, while compounded profit growth is 26% over 5 years and 77% over 3 years. That is strong enough to support the long-term case.

The problem is earnings quality in the latest twelve months. Screener shows other income of Rs 88,669 Cr in TTM, which massively distorts bottom-line profit and makes the TTM net profit figure unusable for normal valuation work. The operating line is more reliable, and on that basis the margin picture has improved materially versus earlier years but has softened recently from 12% in FY25 to 7% in TTM.

Quarterly data also turned weak. The latest quarter on Screener shows sales of Rs 15,268 Cr, operating profit of Rs 689 Cr, OPM of 5%, and net loss of Rs 233 Cr. Yahoo also indicates a weak Q3 FY26 with actual GAAP normalized EPS of -6.27, though Yahoo's revenue basis appears different from Screener.

Conclusion on profitability: long-term growth is acceptable, margins improved materially into FY25, but the latest quarter and TTM figures show clear deterioration and accounting noise.

## 7. Valuation analysis

This is where the case gets messy.

Using Yahoo's market-linked data, TMPV trades at about 29.6 times trailing earnings, 0.29 times sales, 1.04 times book, and 3.91 times EV/EBITDA. Using Screener, the stock trades at 20.7 times earnings and about 4.71 times book. Peer data on Screener shows:

| Company | P/E | ROCE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maruti Suzuki | 26.5 | 21.7% |
| M&M | 22.8 | 13.9% |
| Hyundai Motor India | 28.9 | 54.3% |
| TMPV | 20.7 | 20.3% |

On P/E alone, TMPV does not look expensive relative to large peers. On price-to-book, it is either reasonable or expensive depending on which source is used. Because the book-value mismatch is so large and TTM earnings are distorted by extraordinary other income, the cleanest conclusion is that valuation is not obviously cheap enough to ignore the uncertainty.

For a cyclical, capital-intensive auto business with a weak recent quarter, I would not call the stock clearly undervalued based on the fetched data.

## 8. Moat, management, dividend, and shareholding analysis

Potential moat drivers:

| Area | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Brand | Strong Tata brand in India and premium JLR positioning globally |
| Distribution | Large dealer and service footprint implied by scale and industry position |
| EV position | Meaningful strategic advantage in Indian EV passenger vehicles |
| Scale | Material scale benefits in sourcing, manufacturing, and financing ecosystem |

Dividend profile is reasonable. Both sources show a 1.91% yield, and Screener notes a healthy dividend payout history. That is supportive, but not a core reason to own the stock.

Shareholding is mixed. Screener shows current promoter holding around 46.39%, DIIs around 17.69%, and FIIs around 15.34%. Institutional ownership is meaningful. However, Screener's own machine-generated note warns that promoter holding has decreased by 3.83% over 3 years, while the raw table is harder to interpret because the site also warns that classification may have changed from Sep 2022 onward. I would treat promoter-trend conclusions cautiously rather than call them clearly bullish.

## 9. Risks and red flags

1. Earnings quality is weak in TTM because Rs 88,669 Cr of other income distorts net profit.
2. Latest quarter was weak, with Screener showing a net loss of Rs 233 Cr and OPM falling to 5%.
3. Revenue is materially exposed to global auto demand and JLR, so this is not a simple domestic India consumption bet.
4. Valuation inputs are inconsistent across Yahoo and Screener, especially on P/E, book value, and P/B.
5. The stock has underperformed over 1 year and is trading near its 52-week low, which signals execution concerns or market skepticism.
6. Historical sales growth is decent but not exceptional for a cyclical manufacturer, and Screener explicitly flags 5-year sales growth as weak relative to expectations.

## 10. Final verdict: Neutral for long-term investing

TMPV has several genuine strengths: debt has come down sharply, recent ROE and ROCE are above 15%, operating cash flow improved in the last few reported years, and the business has real scale, brand strength, and global optionality through JLR and EVs. Those are not trivial positives.

The reason the verdict is Neutral rather than Bullish is that current fundamentals are not clean enough. The latest quarter was weak, TTM profit is heavily distorted by extraordinary other income, and major valuation fields do not reconcile between Yahoo Finance and Screener. In a cyclical, capital-intensive sector, that uncertainty matters. The stock does not look obviously overvalued, but it also does not look cheap enough or clean enough to justify a strong long-term bullish call from the fetched data alone.

For a long-term investor, TMPV is worth tracking, not chasing. A cleaner bullish setup would be sustained margin recovery, normalised earnings without extraordinary income distortion, and a few more quarters confirming that cash generation and return ratios remain strong.
